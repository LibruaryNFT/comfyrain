# Consult: how far can a 2D-only WoW weather overlay be pushed?

You are advising on a World of Warcraft addon called ComfyRain. It makes it look and sound
like it is raining (or snowing) whenever the player wants, because the client cannot be told
to change the weather. It ships 4 November on CurseForge.

Answer concretely and rank by impact. If any premise below is wrong, say so first and say
how you know; several of these were established by reading the client binary and by running
checks in a live client, and they are not guesses, but they may still be incomplete.

---

## The hard ceiling, verified

An addon in this client gets **no access** to:

- world lighting, fog, or any post-process
- the depth buffer, the framebuffer, or the camera matrix
- the particle system
- shaders of any kind

What it **does** get:

- `Texture` objects on frames, drawn over the 3D world, in draw layers
  BACKGROUND / BORDER / ARTWORK / OVERLAY
- blend modes `BLEND`, `ADD`, `MOD`, `ALPHAKEY`. MOD can only darken, ADD can only lighten
- `SetTexCoord(l, r, t, b)` with REPEAT wrap, so a tiled sheet can be scrolled by moving
  texture coordinates; this is how the rain falls
- `SetVertexColor`, `SetGradient` (two-colour, vertical or horizontal), `SetAlpha`,
  `SetRotation`, `SetMask`
- `Model` frames, which can load a game `.m2` and render it in their own rectangle. We
  verified `Goblin_Weather_Machine_Rain.m2` (FileDataID 166249) **loads** in one. We have
  **not** established that anything is visible, nor whether a Model frame can be made
  full-screen and transparent-backgrounded, nor whether its particles emit without a
  scene. Treat its usefulness as an open question, not as a yes.
- sound: `PlaySoundFile` / `C_Sound.PlaySoundWithOptions` by FileDataID, with
  `volumeOverride`, on a chosen channel. `MuteSoundFile` can silence Blizzard's own
  ambience files, but it is keyed on the FILE and not the caller, so muting a file also
  mutes us playing that same file.
- `C_Weather.GetCurrentWeather()` — read-only, returns the server's weather type and
  intensity. There is no setter and there never has been.
- CVars. `weatherDensity` (0-3) controls how densely the client draws the weather the
  server sent; at 0 it draws none. Whether an addon may *write* it is being probed this
  week and is currently **unknown**.

Everything runs in Lua 5.1 on an `OnUpdate` that fires each frame. There is no threading.
A frame budget measured in tens of microseconds is the realistic limit.

## What is built today

- Three full-screen scrolling sheets: `mist` (BLEND, BACKGROUND, 1.1 tiles tall),
  `far` (BLEND, ARTWORK, 3.0 tiles), `near` (ADD, ARTWORK, 1.5 tiles). Horizontal tiling is
  derived as `tilesY * aspect` so each tile is square in screen pixels and the baked 7.5
  degree streak slant survives at any resolution.
- The sheets are 512x512 32-bit TGA, generated by our own script from an optical model of a
  motion-blurred drop (nothing of Blizzard's is redistributed). Physical screen is
  2127x1080, so the near layer's tile is 720 screen pixels tall drawn from 512 texels: it is
  magnified 1.4x at normal intensity and 2.4x at the heaviest preset.
- A MOD quad darkens the world; a separate time-of-day system grades towards dusk/night with
  a MOD darken plus a small ADD lift.
- One Ornstein-Uhlenbeck gust process (tau 7 s, sigma 0.35) with per-layer one-pole
  followers, driving scroll rate, layer alpha, slant shear and gloom. This was itself the
  result of a prior consult and is considered settled.
- Lightning: a full-screen white ADD quad flashed for ~0.22 s, with thunder from Blizzard's
  own FileDataIDs at a log-normal interval.
- An ambience bed of Blizzard rain loops, crossfaded, with volume jitter.
- The rain fades out indoors.

## What changed this week, and why we are asking

Blizzard shipped **engine-side lightning** in the same client: real bolts in the sky that
light the environment, driven by a `CWeatherLightningManager` and a `Lightning` DB2 record,
scheduled by the server. There is no Lua surface for it; we cannot trigger it or read it.

So our flat white flash now has a high-fidelity neighbour that players have just seen, and
the overlay has to stand next to it without looking cheap. The product argument is unchanged
(theirs is server-scheduled and rare, ours is on demand and permanent), but the visual bar
moved.

## The question

**Within the ceiling above, what is the highest-impact ordered list of changes that would
make this read as world-class weather rather than as a texture laid over the screen?**

Rank by perceived quality gained per unit of risk, and for each item say what it costs in
frame time and in failure modes. Be specific enough to implement.

Address these directly, and say if any is a dead end:

1. **Depth.** The biggest tell is that the rain is uniformly in front of everything,
   including the player's own character and nearby walls. With no depth buffer, is there
   any honest way to suggest depth? Vignetting, a radial mask, a horizon-weighted alpha
   gradient, a parallax tied to `GetPlayerFacing()` and the camera pitch, something else?
2. **Lightning.** How do we make a flash read as a bolt in the world rather than as the
   screen going white, using only quads, gradients, rotation and masks? Consider: an
   off-centre bright region, a two-stage flash with a leader and a return stroke, coloured
   rather than white, a brief complementary darkening afterwards, a thunder delay that
   encodes distance.
3. **Wet world.** Everything so far is airborne. Is there anything worth doing for the
   ground, the screen surface (droplets on the "lens"), or light bloom, given that we cannot
   read the scene?
4. **Resolution.** Is the 512 to 1024 texture upgrade worth it at 2127x1080, given that we
   ship uncompressed TGA with no mipmaps and a 1024 32-bit sheet is 4.2 MB on disk? Does the
   lack of mipmaps mean the *minified* far layer is the one that actually shimmers?
5. **Standing aside.** When the server does send a real storm, should we suppress our own
   layers entirely, blend with them, or do nothing? What reads best to a player?
6. **What not to do.** Name the things that look appealing here and are actually traps.

Also: we have 19 days to launch, the client disappears for 14 of them, and any change made
during that window cannot be seen running before it ships. Flag anything in your list that
must not be shipped unseen.
